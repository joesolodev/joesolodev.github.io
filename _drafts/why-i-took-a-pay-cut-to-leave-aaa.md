When I say I've gone from AAA to indie, I mean something specific: it's the kind of work I'm purposely looking for.

Today is day 84 of my move from AAA game development to indie. I'm working on my game, and I've been thinking about what that move actually means.

## I went looking for this

I'm working for a studio now. It's an indie studio, and I actively sought them out. I actively sought out taking a pay cut. I actively sought out all kinds of things that would be an adjustment to anybody's life or lifestyle, because that's the space I actually want to be in.

I haven't pursued any AAA character art, or 3D art of any kind. I've been focused on the indie space, because I think that's where things are happening right now. That's where everyone's going. That's where all the fun is.

## Making games should be fun

In AAA, making games wasn't all that fun for me. It made me long for a space where people were having fun and shipping things. Small things, cool things.

Sometimes in AAA, things just take too long. People say indie takes a long time, but AAA has games in development for seven years that never ship.

I've shipped over 12 titles, but I've worked on 23 or 24 in total. That's 11 or 12 games I put time, energy and effort into that never saw the light of day.

There are people who got their first job on a game, spent seven years on it, and then watched it get canceled. Their entire industry experience might be seven years at an associate level, with nothing shipped to show for it.

## The new perks win

So I jumped ship to indie on purpose. That has meant making less money and having fewer perks.

But the new perks outweigh the old ones by a mile. I'm even able to work on my own game now, and I think that's amazing.

That's the difference between a AAA contractor and an indie contractor. I'm not looking for that kind of work anymore.
