Shipshape Validator

Check your model before it leaves Blender, so it arrives in Unreal or Unity the way you expect.

Shipshape looks over a character or a prop, tells you in plain language what will break in the engine, offers a fix where one is safe, and exports with settings that are known to work.

It is a safety net for the export step. It is not a general mesh cleanup tool and not a rigging tool — it is deliberately narrow, and it says so.

You exported it. It arrived wrong. Now what?

The character is lying on its side. Or it’s the size of a coin. Or it imported fine but the arms tear apart the moment it animates. Or it’s just… grey.

None of those tell you what actually went wrong, and Blender never warned you. By the time the engine complains, you’re guessing which of a dozen things caused it.

Shipshape checks the model before it leaves. It looks over your character or prop, tells you in plain language what will break, offers a fix where one is safe, and exports with settings that are known to work.

Installing

  1. In Blender, go to Edit → Preferences → Add-ons.
  2. Click the arrow at the top right, then Install from Disk.
  3. Pick shipshape_validator-1.0.0.zip.
  4. It turns on by itself. Close preferences.

Press N in the 3D viewport to open the sidebar. There is now a Shipshape tab down the right-hand edge.

The five minute version

  1. Click your character’s skeleton (or your prop’s mesh).
  2. Press Check.
  3. Read the list. Red will break; yellow is worth a look.
  4. Press a fix button, or follow the steps under How to fix it.
  5. When it says Ready for Unreal, press Export.

That is the whole tool. Everything below is detail.

Select and Check

Each finding shows:

  • What is wrong, in a sentence, with the number that matters — “3 bones sit at the top with no parent (pelvis, ik_foot_root, ik_hand_root)”
  • Select — jumps to the exact bones, vertices, edges or faces in question
  • A fix button, when there is one that is safe to apply
  • What is this? — expands into what the thing is, why the engine cares, and what to do differently next time
  • How to fix it — step-by-step, on findings that have no fix button
  • An eye icon — mark this finding as intentional

Guided Export

Three buttons: Static Prop, Character, Animation.

Pick what you are exporting and Shipshape checks everything that matters for it in one go — for a character, that is the skeleton and every mesh bound to it — then shows a per-object summary. Press Review on any row to see the detail.

Use this if you are not sure which object to select. It works that out for you, and it finds the right skeleton on a Rigify or Game Rig Tools setup rather than the control rig.

Something looks wrong in Unreal

Work backwards. You imported something, it looks wrong, and you do not know which of twenty things caused it.

Pick the symptom you can see:

  • My model is tiny or gigantic
  • My model is lying on its side
  • My model is grey and has no textures
  • My mesh is spiky, or parts are stuck at the origin
  • It imported in T-pose and won’t animate
  • There are extra bones I didn’t make
  • Unreal said “multiple roots”
  • The model looks inside-out or see-through
  • I can walk through the model
  • A camera or light came through with it

Shipshape shows the checks that cause that symptom, and runs them on your file.

Some symptoms are not caused by anything in your .blend at all. Missing textures and missing collision get a written explanation of what to do in the engine instead, because that is where the answer is.

What it checks

Twenty-five checks. Every one of them explains itself.

Skeleton 9 checks

  • ✓ Scale applied
  • ✓ Rotation applied
  • ✓ One root bone
  • ✓ Root bone at the world origin
  • ✓ Something is actually bound to it
  • ✓ Not a control rig you shouldn’t be exporting
  • ✓ Deform bones no longer driven by live constraints
  • ✓ Bones per vertex within the engine’s limit
  • ✓ Bone names suited to Unity’s Humanoid rig (Unity only)

Mesh 10 checks

  • ✓ Scale applied
  • ✓ Rotation applied
  • ✓ A sensible real-world size
  • ✓ Bound to the right skeleton
  • ✓ Non-manifold geometry
  • ✓ Inconsistent normals
  • ✓ Inverted normals (inside-out models)
  • ✓ Non-flat n-gons
  • ✓ Zero-area faces
  • ✓ Loose geometry

Weights 3 checks

  • ✓ Weight totals normalized
  • ✓ Vertices with no weight
  • ✓ Vertex groups matching real bones

Materials 3 checks

  • ✓ A material is assigned
  • ✓ Image textures rather than procedural ones
  • ✓ Texture files actually present on disk

It understands Rigify and Game Rig Tools setups specifically — it finds the rig you actually export, not the control rig.

Fixes that don’t wreck your rig

Apply scale. Recalculate normals. Bake and clear constraints. Add a root bone. Limit bone influences. Normalize weights. Triangulate, then spin the edges to choose how it splits. Delete loose geometry. Fix All Safe Issues applies every safe one in a single click.

All undoable with Ctrl+Z. But more importantly:

Every fix tells you what it does not promise. Applying scale keeps the character the size it currently looks — so if it was tiny, it’s still tiny, just baked in. The tool says so, next to the button and again after it runs.

Fixes refuse rather than guess. Applying a transform over a posed rig would bake the pose into the rest pose, so it stops and explains. Baking animation with nothing to bake would freeze the rig, so it stops and explains. Adding a root bone never moves an existing one.

And some things deliberately have no fix button. Nothing here will guess which bone an unweighted vertex belongs to, or rescale your character for you. Those get instructions instead.

Fix All

When more than one safe fix is available, a Fix All Safe Issues button appears above the list. It applies them together and re-checks.

It only ever runs fixes that are safe unattended. Anything that could lose work — deleting a vertex group that still holds weights, trimming bone influences, triangulating — stays a deliberate single button press, and Fix All leaves it alone. So does anything you have marked intentional.

When a warning is wrong

Some rigs break the rules on purpose. A skeleton might carry a scale that looks wrong and is not.

Click the eye icon on that finding. It stops counting: it leaves the list, stops blocking export, and Fix All ignores it.

Marked findings are never hidden. They stay listed under Marked intentional, with a button to bring them back, and the green summary says “22 passed, 1 marked intentional” rather than pretending everything passed.

The mark is saved in the .blend, per object. Ignoring something on one character does not silence it on another, and it survives closing the file.

A few checks refuse to be marked — the ones that always break the export.

Exporting

Choose Engine — Unreal or Unity — then press Export.

Shipshape uses a preset that is known to work with that engine, so you never see the FBX dialog. Leaf bones off, deform bones only, no cameras or lights, correct smoothing and tangents, textures copied alongside. Press What does the export do? to read exactly what it sets, in plain language. Nothing is hidden.

These weren’t guessed from forum posts. Both presets were verified by importing into the real engine and reading back what the engine produced — Unreal 5.8 and Unity 6. Correct size, correct orientation, correct skeleton, measured rather than eyeballed.

Export is blocked while there are red errors, and the button says how many. Warnings do not block. If you need to export anyway, tick Export anyway, despite errors in the file browser.

The engine choice is saved with your .blend, because a project targets one engine.

Frequently asked

Why is my model grey in the engine?

FBX carries the shape and the bones, not Blender materials — it only records where your image textures live. Import those images in the engine and build a material there. Anything procedural (noise, bricks, checkers) doesn’t survive the trip at all and needs baking to an image first. Shipshape warns about both, including the silent case where a procedural material exports as a blank one and looks like it worked.

It says my character is 2cm tall. Is that a bug?

No — it really is. Applying a transform bakes in whatever size the model looks right now, so a rig that was scaled down stays small. Every other check passes and the character is still unusable in-engine. Scale it up, apply, check again.

It flagged my rig and my rig is fine.

Possible, and worth telling me about. Mark it intentional with the eye icon to get on with your work, then send me the file. Several real bugs were found exactly that way — including one where a correct UE4 mannequin rig was being told to break itself.

Does it work with my rig?

Rigify, Game Rig Tools, Auto-Rig Pro, Mixamo, and plain hand-built armatures. It recognises which one it’s looking at and adjusts — a raw Rigify control rig gets told to generate a game rig rather than being buried in 200 complaints about its control bones.

Do I have to run it on every object?

No. Use Guided Export and it gathers everything for you.

Can I export with warnings showing?

Yes. Only red errors block, and even those can be overridden.
