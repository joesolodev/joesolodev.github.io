A question I've been asked a lot over the last 20 years is, "Should I become a character artist?" My answer is usually no.

Becoming a character artist is really hard, especially if you're starting from zero. It's pretty much the hardest thing I've ever done in my life.

Would I go back and do it all over again? I would, except next time I'd be a concept artist. I feel like I settled for character art because I couldn't quite do concept art, but that's neither here nor there.

## What the work actually takes

Most people misunderstand the actual work that goes into being a real character artist. There are tons of tutorials online that make you think you don't need to learn things like drawing, painting and sculpting with real materials. You do.

And you don't just have to learn how to do it. You need to be able to do it at a mid to high level. You can't just say, "Oh, I've drawn a couple of things, now I'm going to be a character artist." No.

- Do you understand what a gesture is?
- Do you understand what a one-two lighting blockout is?
- Can you do a gesture in 10 minutes?
- Can you do a 20-minute face lay-in with just a pencil?
- Can you do an alla prima oil painting in two and a half to three hours?

If the answer is no, and you don't want to put in the time, energy and effort to be able to do those things, you don't actually want to be a character artist. And I don't advise you to be one. That's really what it comes down to.

## When my answer is yes

Now, if you already do all those things, if you're already dope at 2D, especially in a non-digital, analog medium, then yeah, I might tell you that you should become a character artist.

I have met a few people like that. One or two in 20 years. I've even tried to pair them up with mentors immediately, because yeah, I'll help you. If your work's good, you've already put the time, energy and effort into one thing, and those skills are transferable.

## It takes a lifetime

If you want to be a character artist, you need to be willing to work on becoming a masterful artist for the rest of your life. Being a real artist of any kind requires the dedication of a lifetime.

It's not a montage where you get through it, you're done, and all your dreams come true. You have to always be evolving, always be changing, always be learning. And you have to understand that you're always in competition with somebody else.

## Get mad, or be relieved

I don't want to discourage anybody from doing anything. In fact, if this made you mad, I hope you get mad and become a super dope character artist.

If this made you happy, if this was a relief to you, awesome too.
